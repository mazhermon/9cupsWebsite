# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

House music heads and UK Garage fans. Most arrive on mobile from a link drop: Instagram bio, story tap-through, share-from-friend, release-post link. Some are existing fans coming back to mess with stems; many are first-touch listeners taking 30 to 90 seconds to decide whether 9cups is worth a follow.

State of mind is curious-but-not-committed. Headphones may or may not be on. They will not read more than a few words. They will tap, listen, and decide.

## Product Purpose

A creative, interactive showcase that lets visitors play with the multitrack stems of a 9cups release in real time, then sends them to the platforms where they can stream or buy the finished record. Not a tool, not a utility, a brand experience. The mixer is the hook; the platform CTAs are the conversion.

Success looks like a visitor staying through at least one full loop of the track, muting and unmuting a stem, leaving with the vibe stuck in their head, and clicking through to Spotify, Apple Music, Bandcamp, or SoundCloud. Bonus signal: they share the link.

This is the first experiment for the 9cups site. The site will grow around this section later; the goal here is one impeccable hero, not a complete site.

## Brand Personality

The Wise Enjoyer. Contemplative, communal, psychedelic without ego, knowing, unhurried, layered, Wellington-rooted, globally influenced. Confident, generous, present, never striving.

Voice for this surface: literary not corporate, present not performing, warm not precious. Old-style serif for headings, quiet sans for support. Deep purples, warm magenta accents, never flat colour, always grain.

Source of truth for the visual identity lives in `.claude/skills/9cups-brand/`. Defer to that canon when in doubt.

## Anti-references

- Soundcloud's beat editor and any "DAW-in-a-browser" aesthetic.
- Generic "audio reactive" demos: neon-on-pure-black, glitchy retrowave, hyperdistorted blobs. The site's first version drifted here; it gets unwound.
- Splice, Native Instruments, BandLab product UI.
- Tech-bro startup landing pages: centered hero, gradient orb, "modern" geometric sans.
- Editorial-magazine pastiche: italic display serif, ruled separators, mono labels. Wrong lane for a music brand.
- Pure minimalism. The brand calls for layered and textured; minimalism reads cold.

## Design Principles

1. **Texture is voice.** Never flat colour. Grain, paper fibre, asphalt, mandala, at least one texture must bleed through every surface. The grain says this music came from somewhere real.
2. **Among, not above.** The page sits next to the visitor, not over them. Intimate over spectacular. Compositions breathe.
3. **Layered reveal.** Like the brand's double-exposure photography, interaction should uncover something. The longer you play, the more the page rewards you.
4. **Confident generosity.** The brand has nothing to prove. Type sets at full size, music breathes, the mixer doesn't beg you to stay.
5. **Smooth or it doesn't ship.** Janky animation breaks trust faster than missing features. Anything reactive must hold 60fps on a mid-tier Android phone, or it gets a different technique.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.2 AA across colour contrast, focus indication, keyboard navigation.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` users see static, brand-aligned shapes. No displacement, no scaling, no movement. Audio still plays; mute state still reads visually.
- Audio-driven feedback always has a non-audio twin. A deaf or hard-of-hearing visitor must be able to tell which stems are playing from visual state alone.
- Touch targets at least 44 by 44px. Focus rings at least 3px and high-contrast against any background a control sits on.
- All controls keyboard-operable. Screen-reader labels written in the brand's voice, not template defaults.
