Visual Lineage & Reference

A curated research document mapping the visual territory adjacent to the 9Cups brand — artists, designers, and design principles to learn from and be in conversation with. Not to copy, but to understand the neighbourhood.

For reference and learning only · All named works belong to their respective creators
01

Adjacent Artists — Visual DNA

Electronic artists whose visual identity shares territory with 9Cups — warmth, layering, spiritual depth, the personal over the spectacular. Study what they're doing, then ask how 9Cups does it differently.

Bonobo
Highest relevance

Simon Green's visual world — shot primarily by Neil Krug and designed by Leif Podhajsky — shares the most DNA with 9Cups. Photographic, textured, atmospheric. Landscapes become dreamscapes. Motion and transience as recurring themes. The music and the image feel like the same thing.

What to learn
  • Photography as the raw material, not illustration
  • Colour temperature signals the emotional register of each release
  • The same visual language can be cool/introspective OR warm/celebratory
  • Nature imagery layered with colour wash — organic + psychedelic
  • Bonobo's "Fragments" concept: motion, transience, tides — cycles as theme
Maya Jane Coles
High relevance

Self-made visual identity — she draws, paints, and makes her own tour visuals from sketchbooks kept on the road. The intimacy and personal quality of doing it yourself runs through everything. Her Night Creature era: poster-board aesthetic, techno precision with warmth underneath.

What to learn
  • The power of a personally-made visual vs outsourced design
  • Sketchbook quality — rough, gestural, not over-finished
  • Poster-within-poster layout: frame-within-frame thinking
  • Dark foundation with warm human elements breaking through
Flying Lotus
Medium relevance

Psychedelia and spirituality as a consistent throughline — deeply influenced by Alice Coltrane and cosmic jazz. His visuals merge fluid organic shapes with angular machine-like structures. Surreal and disorienting. The visual world is eclectic but always unmistakably his.

What to learn
  • Spirituality as a visual language, not just a mood
  • Genre-hopping can coexist with a consistent visual identity
  • Organic and mechanical layered together — not either/or
  • 3D and flat combined: depth without realism
Floating Points
Medium relevance

Sam Shepherd's visual world pulls from jazz spirituality (Alice Coltrane, Sun Ra) and translates it into abstract projections and live visual collaborations. His Cascade tour used real-time abstract projections by Hamill Industries — visuals that feel improvised, alive, present.

What to learn
  • Live energy captured in still form — presence over polish
  • Abstract projection as extension of the music's feeling
  • Jazz influence as visual cue: warmth, flow, unpredictability
  • Collaboration with visual artists as part of the brand identity
Tipper
Reference only

Ambient organic sounds with psychedelic creature visuals by Android Jones. A study in how an abstract electronic artist builds a visual world around creatures, swirling patterns, and neon colour. More intense and alien than 9Cups — but the commitment to a total visual experience is instructive.

What to learn
  • Visual collaborators as co-authors of brand identity
  • The psychedelic visual field: swirling, layered, dense
  • Warm vs cool temperature used to signal set mood
  • What NOT to do: 9Cups is warm and grounded, Tipper is alien and intense
02

The Designers — Craft References

The photographers and visual artists who have built the vocabulary we're working adjacent to. Study their techniques, not their outputs.

"I try and work with artists whose music I would want to listen to. Ideally I like to spend a lot of time with the music working out a story and concept which I can capture the whole album's feel and flow." — Leif Podhajsky
Neil Krug
neilkrug.com

The photographer behind Bonobo's Migration and Fragments, Tame Impala's The Slow Rush, Lana Del Rey's Ultraviolence. His technique: psychedelic analogue photography, warm high-key colour, desert landscapes, the feeling of a world "disorientingly dislocated from time." He describes his subjects as being in "memory fragments of places I used to explore."

Relevance to 9Cups
  • Photographic layering with strong colour cast as the primary technique
  • Real locations transformed by light and colour treatment — not composite CGI
  • Analogue grain and imperfection as quality, not flaw
  • Two words guide a shoot: find the emotional brief ("beautifully sinister" for Bonobo)
  • The subject doesn't need to be identifiable — mood matters more than clarity
Leif Podhajsky
leifpodhajsky.com

Australian-born designer behind Tame Impala's Innerspeaker and Lonerism, multiple Bonobo releases. Mixed-media: digital images layered with painted textures and drawings. "Striking abstractions of nature — mirrored vistas, engulfing waves, rippling, melting cosmic landscapes." Has synesthesia — literally sees colour in sound.

Relevance to 9Cups
  • Nature photography as raw material for abstraction — not illustration
  • Recursion and reflection: the Droste effect (image within image) as a technique
  • Colour as emotion: synesthetic approach — what colour does this music feel like?
  • Psychedelic but grounded — surreal but universal, not alien
  • The themes: connectedness, love, nature, altered experience
The Fashtons (Ben Ashton & Fiona Garden)
Visual collaborators for Maya Jane Coles

The creative duo behind Maya Jane Coles' visual identity. They work in intimate settings, often shooting in darkness, using alternative light sources, distortion, and water reflection. "We've bounced her reflection off of water, we've distorted her and fed it back into the image." The intimacy allows the creativity to flow.

Relevance to 9Cups
  • Intimate scale: minimal setups, everything made from scratch
  • Alternative light sources as a compositional tool
  • Portraiture as abstraction — the subject becomes a visual element
  • Long-term collaboration creates a visual shorthand over time
Android Jones
Tipper's visual collaborator / festival VJ

VJ and digital artist working with Tipper, Bassnectar, Grateful Dead. "I was just trying to give a visual form to the languages that he was communicating." His approach: be of service to the music. The visuals are a 4th-dimensional sound hologram translated to 2D. Improvised, responsive, present.

Relevance to 9Cups
  • Visuals as translation of music, not decoration of it
  • Improvisation and presence over pre-planned execution
  • The visual artist in service to the music — ego-free approach
  • Festival scale: tapestries, mandala patterns, large-format immersive art
03

Extracted Principles

What these artists and designers have in common — and what 9Cups can crystallise into its own brand rules.

I
The emotional brief comes first

Krug's "beautifully sinister" for Bonobo. Podhajsky spending time with the music before making a mark. The visual direction starts with two or three words, not a spec. For 9Cups: present, warm, layered.

II
Real world, transformed

Every artist here uses real photography — real places, real objects, real light — as the starting material. The transformation is the art: colour wash, layering, recursion. Not illustration, not CGI. The ground is always real.

III
Colour temperature = emotional register

Bonobo's warm Mojave sunset vs cool underwater Fragments. Flying Lotus's cosmic blue-blacks vs earthy warm tones. The same artist, different releases, radically different colour temperature. Each one signals the mood before a note is heard.

IV
Grain and imperfection as authenticity

Krug's analogue feel, Podhajsky's painted textures bled into digital images, Maya Jane Coles' sketchbook scans. The roughness communicates humanity. Over-polish reads as corporate. The seam, the grain, the scratch — these are features.

V
One recurring symbol anchors everything

Deadmau5's head. Marshmello's helmet. The Nine of Cups figure for 9Cups. A single unmistakable mark that appears across all contexts and acts as a visual shorthand for the whole world. Everything else can change — the logo mark holds.

VI
The intimate perspective

Maya Jane Coles shot from within the crowd. Krug shooting before dawn with only his crew present. Podhajsky in a converted stable in Shoreditch making things. The best music visual art feels like it came from someone's private world — not a studio brief.

VII
Nature as psychedelic raw material

Leaves, water, sand, rock — natural forms treated with colour and light become otherworldly without becoming artificial. The 9Cups leaf canopy. Krug's desert at dawn. Podhajsky's mirrored mountain vistas. Nature is always the starting point.

VIII
Personal philosophy expressed visually

Podhajsky on connectedness and losing touch with nature. Krug on bygone California as "a place in our minds." 9Cups on the Wish Card — quiet abundance, the moment you're already in. The visual work is a philosophy made visible, not just decoration.

04

Learn From vs Avoid

Mapping the territory clearly — what's adjacent enough to learn from, and what's in the wrong neighbourhood entirely.

Draw from these traditions
Analogue-feeling photography with colour treatment
Double exposure and layer transparency as metaphor
Natural textures: sand, leaf, fabric, concrete
Warm, high-key colour without being saccharine
Frame-within-frame compositional thinking
A single recurring symbol as brand anchor
Grain and imperfection communicating authenticity
Spiritual iconography used with cultural respect
The intimate perspective — among, not above
Colour temperature shifts to signal release mood
Typography that feels unhurried and archaic
Wrong neighbourhood
EDM spectacle aesthetics — laser shows, LED helmets
Cold, high-contrast black/white with neon accents
Corporate DJ website templates
Alien/creature visuals (Tipper territory)
Futuristic tech aesthetics — glitch, cyberpunk, circuit boards
Hero shots from above the crowd
Flat illustration / vector art style
Sans-serif everywhere, no typographic warmth
Aggressive drop-shadow and bevel treatment
Stock photography — unnamed places, generic people
Purple gradient on white (generic AI aesthetic)
05

Go Look at These

Direct links to portfolios, articles, and visual archives worth spending time in.

Neil Krug — Portfolio
neilkrug.com

Browse the full portfolio — especially the Bonobo section and the Phantom series. Note the colour temperature in each project, how he uses haze and dust, and the way subjects exist in environments rather than being placed against backdrops.

Leif Podhajsky — Studio
leifpodhajsky.com

Look at the Bonobo work (North Borders, Cirrus) and the Tame Impala Innerspeaker series. Notice the recursion and mirroring techniques, how natural landscapes become abstract fields, and how the colour palette serves as the emotional carrier.

Creative Review — Bonobo Fragments
creativereview.co.uk

A deep dive into how Neil Krug and Bonobo briefed and made the Fragments campaign. Particularly useful: the concept of "transience, motion, tides and cycles" as a visual brief, and how lenticular printing was used to make images lap against each other like waves.

Leif Podhajsky — WePresent Interview
wepresent.wetransfer.com

The best interview about his process. Key quote: "A little bit surreal, but universal." How he uses confusion as a gateway into engagement. How he spends time with the music before touching a tool. His concept of album art as a "story which encompasses an entire album's feel and flow."

Neil Krug — WePresent
wepresent.wetransfer.com

On memory fragments, landscape, and abstraction. "It's either the inexplicable, or the complete direct, or the complete abstract." How his mind hops between a landscape, a portrait, and pure colour — and how those three registers work together as a series.

Fabric London — Maya Jane Coles & The Fashtons
fabriclondon.com

On the intimacy of small-scale shoots and what it enables creatively. "We've done so many experiments with alternative light sources that it's become a running joke that we always seem to be shooting in the dark." The value of a long-term visual collaborator.
