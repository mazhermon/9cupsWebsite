Typography rules
Wordmark and release titles always in IM Fell English. Feels slightly archaic, literary — never corporate.
Body text in DM Sans light (300) — complements the serif without competing. Never bold body text.
Italic for mood lines, quotes, tag phrases. The italic version of IM Fell English has real character — use it.
All caps used sparingly, widely spaced, small size only — meta information like dates and locations.

Texture rules
Never use flat colour fills without at least one texture layer bleeding through. The grain is part of the voice.
The double-exposure technique is the core brand signature — real photography overlaid with colour washes. The seams are visible intentionally.
Tapestry/mandala patterns (as seen at Newtown Fest) — intricate, global, celebratory. Available as a background source.
Paper/screen grain on the tarot cards — sketch-like, archaic, literary. Use when referencing the Nine of Cups figure.
Key symbols
The Nine of Cups figure — primary logo mark. The smiling, seated figure with nine chalices in an arc. Always used for profile/avatar contexts. Never replaced with a photograph.
Two tin cups on a woven mat — communion, sharing, campfire. The brand sub-motif for release art and social.
The mandala tapestry — intricate, global, celebratory. Background and texture source for live/festival contexts.
The Wise Enjoyer
Not the seeker (still searching). Not the hero (striving). The figure in the Nine of Cups — someone who has found something real, knows it, and wants to share it generously. Satisfaction without smugness. Abundance without showing off.

Spatial language
Intimate, not spectacular. Even festival photography is shot from within the crowd — never above it.
Perspective is always among, never above.
Compositions breathe. Objects placed with care — two cups on a mat is a still life.
Frame-within-frame / rectangle-within-square gives a printmaking quality. This brand thinks about the frame.
